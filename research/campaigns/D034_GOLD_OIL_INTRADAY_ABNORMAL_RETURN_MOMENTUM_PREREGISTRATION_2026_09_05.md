## Source
Caporale, G.M. & Plastun, A. (2021), *Gold and oil prices: abnormal returns, momentum and contrarian effects*, Financial Markets and Portfolio Management 35, 353–368. DOI `10.1007/s11408-021-00380-w`.

The paper uses daily/hourly MetaQuotes gold and oil data, defines abnormal returns using a dynamic threshold with `k=2` standard deviations, estimates intraday times at which an abnormal day becomes identifiable, and for Strategy 1 opens in the direction of the abnormal return and closes at end-of-day. Reported Strategy-1 success rates are around 70% on average and profits are positive/significant in the source sample; source trading simulations exclude transaction costs.

Source timing parameters used here:
- GOLD positive abnormal return: after 17:00;
- GOLD negative abnormal return: after 19:00;
- OIL positive abnormal return: after 16:00;
- OIL negative abnormal return: after 19:00;
- source research clock: GMT+3.

## Why this is labeled ADAPTATION, not exact replication
The accessible article specifies a dynamic threshold `mean_n +/- 2*sd_n` but does not pin down a causal live-trading lookback `n` clearly enough for an exact historical reproduction. The paper's timing parameters are also estimated from its whole historical sample, and the FundedNext tester exposes server timestamps rather than a guaranteed historical fixed GMT+3 clock.

D034 therefore freezes a causal implementation BEFORE testing:
- prior **252 completed D1 open-to-close returns** form the dynamic reference distribution;
- sample mean and sample standard deviation;
- upper threshold = `mean252 + 2*sd252`;
- lower threshold = `mean252 - 2*sd252`;
- current-day return = current midpoint / first midpoint quote of current broker day - 1;
- only prior completed days are used, never future data;
- the source timing hour is applied to the tester/server calendar day with no post-result clock shift.

No alternate 60/120/500-day threshold is allowed to rescue the same development result. Diagnostic z-scores for shorter windows may be logged passively only.

## Frozen Strategy 1 execution
For each broker/server calendar day:
1. capture first executable midpoint quote as day open;
2. compute frozen causal 252-day abnormal thresholds from prior completed D1 open-to-close returns;
3. after the source market/direction timing hour, if current cumulative day return first crosses the positive threshold, open LONG at contemporaneous ASK;
4. after the source market/direction timing hour, if current cumulative day return first crosses the negative threshold, open SHORT at contemporaneous BID;
5. maximum one Strategy-1 entry per day;
6. no SL, TP, trailing or Guardian;
7. close at the **last observed executable quote of that same calendar day**; LONG exits BID, SHORT exits ASK;
8. spread is therefore embedded; no overnight swap should be generated by the intended same-day rule; historical commission is not reconstructed in v1.00.

Because the source has no natural stop, do NOT invent R. Primary metrics are executable bps/percent, win rate, MFE/MAE bps and normalized excursion diagnostics.

## Advancement gate
Gold and Oil are evaluated separately first, then pooled only as a secondary view. A market advances only if all are true:
1. at least 40 fully resolved clean Strategy-1 events;
2. mean executable return > `+15 bps/event` after embedded spread;
3. median executable bps > 0;
4. month-block bootstrap 95% lower bound of mean executable bps > 0;
5. positive and negative abnormal-return directions both have positive mean executable bps if each has at least 10 events;
6. at least two of calendar 2024, 2025 and 2026 have positive mean executable bps;
7. no single event contributes >20% of total positive executable bps.

`+15 bps/event` is deliberately demanding because the project needs a materially useful prop-firm engine rather than merely reproducing a statistically significant academic anomaly.

A FAIL closes this exact D034 implementation. Do not rescue the same sample by moving timing hours, changing `k=2`, changing 252 days, adding RSI/session filters, or adding stops/targets.
